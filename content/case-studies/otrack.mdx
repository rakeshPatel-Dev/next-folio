## Project Overview

Ghardailo Track is an internal order and delivery management system built for Ghardailo Dairy.

The system helps staff manage bulk customer orders, schedule deliveries across different dates and times, manage products, and monitor daily operations from a centralized dashboard.

A key requirement was flexibility: a single order can contain multiple products, with different quantities of the same or different products scheduled for different delivery dates.

---

## The Problem

The existing order workflow relied heavily on manual tracking, making it difficult to:

- Manage bulk customer orders efficiently
- Track deliveries scheduled for different dates
- See what products are required for today's deliveries
- Monitor pending and late deliveries
- Maintain consistent product pricing
- Get a quick overview of daily sales and operations

The system needed to reduce this operational friction without making the ordering process complicated for staff.

---

## Goals

The primary goals were to:

- Centralize order and delivery management
- Support flexible, multi-date delivery schedules
- Make order creation fast and straightforward
- Provide clear daily operational visibility
- Reduce manual calculation and tracking
- Create a foundation that can be extended as the business grows

## Core Features

### Order Management

Orders can contain multiple delivery schedules, allowing products from the same order to be delivered on different dates.

Each delivery schedule tracks:

- Product
- Quantity
- Delivery date
- Delivery time
- Delivery slot
- Notes

Order totals are calculated automatically from the scheduled products and quantities.

### Flexible Delivery Scheduling

A major design decision was to make delivery schedules the operational unit rather than forcing an order to have a single delivery date.

For example, a customer can place one bulk order where:

- 100 units are delivered today
- 50 units are delivered tomorrow
- 100 units are delivered next week

All of these remain part of the same order while appearing independently in daily delivery views.

### Product Management

Frequently ordered products are maintained centrally with predefined pricing and unit types.

This makes order creation faster and helps maintain consistent product information across orders.

### Operations Dashboard

The dashboard provides a quick view of the day's operations, including:

- Orders and deliveries for today
- Pending and late deliveries
- Today's sales
- Product quantities required for delivery
- Tomorrow's delivery requirements
- Due collections
- Recent activity
- Top customers and products

### Sales & Delivery Trends

The dashboard includes lightweight operational trends:

- **Sales trend** — 7-day / 30-day revenue visualization with total, average, best day, and period comparison.
- **Delivery volume** — delivery activity over time with operational KPIs.
- **Top products** — visual breakdown of the products being delivered most frequently.

### Reports

The reporting section provides broader analysis of business activity across selectable periods.

It focuses on:

- Revenue trends
- Product performance
- Customer activity
- Delivery performance
- Order activity

This allows the business to move beyond individual orders and understand operational patterns over time.

### Role-Based Access

The system supports two roles:

- **Admin** — manages staff, products, and system-level operations.
- **Staff** — performs day-to-day order and delivery operations based on assigned permissions.

Authentication uses JWT-based sessions with HttpOnly cookies.

---

## Key Design Decision

### Delivery Schedules Instead of Separate Orders

One of the main architectural decisions was keeping delivery schedules embedded within an order.

Instead of creating separate orders whenever a customer wants deliveries on different dates, a single order can contain multiple delivery schedules.

This preserves the relationship between the customer's original order and its individual deliveries while allowing the dashboard to query schedules independently for daily operations.

This was particularly important for bulk orders where quantities are often distributed across multiple delivery dates.

**Backend**

**Architecture**

The application follows a separated frontend/backend architecture with MongoDB as the primary data store.

The backend handles authentication, authorization, business rules, calculations, and data aggregation, while the frontend focuses on operational workflows and visualization.

## What I Learned

This project pushed me beyond simply building CRUD interfaces.

The biggest challenge was translating a real business workflow into a data model that remained simple for staff to use.

The delivery scheduling requirement was particularly important because the initial approach of treating an order as having a single delivery date did not reflect how bulk orders actually worked.

Designing the system around flexible delivery schedules made the application better aligned with the real operational process.
