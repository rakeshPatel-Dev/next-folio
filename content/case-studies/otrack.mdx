## Project Overview

Otrack is an internal order and delivery management system built for Ghardailo Dairy.

It helps staff manage bulk customer orders, schedule deliveries across different dates and times, manage products, and monitor daily operations from a centralized dashboard.

A key requirement was flexibility. A single order can contain multiple products, with different quantities of the same or different products scheduled for different delivery dates.

---

## The Problem

The existing order workflow relied heavily on manual tracking, which made it hard to:

- Manage bulk customer orders efficiently
- Track deliveries scheduled for different dates
- See what products are required for today's deliveries
- Monitor pending and late deliveries
- Maintain consistent product pricing
- Get a quick overview of daily sales and operations

The system needed to cut down that operational friction without making the ordering process harder for staff.

---

## Goals

The primary goals were to:

- Centralize order and delivery management
- Support flexible, multi-date delivery schedules
- Make order creation fast and straightforward
- Give clear daily operational visibility
- Reduce manual calculation and tracking
- Build a foundation that can grow with the business

## Core Features

### Order Management

Orders can contain multiple delivery schedules, so products from the same order can be delivered on different dates.

Each delivery schedule tracks:

- Product
- Quantity
- Delivery date
- Delivery time
- Delivery slot
- Notes

Order totals are calculated automatically from the scheduled products and quantities.

### Flexible Delivery Scheduling

A major design decision was making delivery schedules the operational unit, rather than forcing an order to have a single delivery date.

For example, a customer can place one bulk order where:

- 100 units are delivered today
- 50 units are delivered tomorrow
- 100 units are delivered next week

All of these stay part of the same order while showing up independently in daily delivery views.

<Callout variant="note" title="Why this matters">
  Treating delivery schedules as the operational unit lets the dashboard
  report on *today's* deliveries without having to split or copy orders.
</Callout>

### Product Management

Frequently ordered products are maintained centrally with predefined pricing and unit types.

This makes order creation faster and keeps product information consistent across orders.

### Operations Dashboard

The dashboard gives a quick view of the day's operations, including:

- Orders and deliveries for today
- Pending and late deliveries
- Today's sales
- Product quantities required for delivery
- Tomorrow's delivery requirements
- Due collections
- Recent activity
- Top customers and products

### Sales & Delivery Trends

The dashboard includes lightweight operational trends:

- **Sales trend** — 7-day and 30-day revenue visualization with total, average, best day, and period comparison.
- **Delivery volume** — delivery activity over time with operational KPIs.
- **Top products** — visual breakdown of the products being delivered most frequently.

### Reports

The reporting section gives broader analysis of business activity across selectable periods.

It focuses on:

- Revenue trends
- Product performance
- Customer activity
- Delivery performance
- Order activity

This lets the business move beyond individual orders and understand operational patterns over time.

### Role-Based Access

The system supports two roles:

- **Admin** manages staff, products, and system-level operations.
- **Staff** handles day-to-day order and delivery operations based on assigned permissions.

| Permission | Admin | Staff |
| --- | :---: | :---: |
| Manage products & pricing | Yes | No |
| Create / edit orders | Yes | Assigned |
| Run daily operations | Yes | Yes |
| View reports & trends | Yes | Limited |

<Callout variant="note">
  Authentication uses JWT-based sessions with HttpOnly cookies, so tokens are
  never exposed to client-side JavaScript.
</Callout>

## Key Design Decision

### Delivery Schedules Instead of Separate Orders

One of the main architectural decisions was keeping delivery schedules embedded inside an order.

Instead of creating separate orders whenever a customer wants deliveries on different dates, a single order can hold multiple delivery schedules.

That preserves the link between the customer's original order and its individual deliveries, while still letting the dashboard query schedules independently for daily operations.

This mattered most for bulk orders, where quantities are usually spread across multiple delivery dates.

**Backend**

- Node.js
- Express.js

- Mongoose
- REST APIs

**Architecture**

The app follows a separated frontend and backend architecture, with MongoDB as the primary data store.

The backend handles authentication, authorization, business rules, calculations, and data aggregation. The frontend focuses on operational workflows and visualization.

## What I Learned

This project pushed me past just building CRUD interfaces.

The biggest challenge was translating a real business workflow into a data model that stayed simple for staff to use.

The delivery scheduling requirement mattered most, because the first approach of treating an order as having a single delivery date didn't reflect how bulk orders actually worked.

Designing the system around flexible delivery schedules made the app fit the real operational process much better.
